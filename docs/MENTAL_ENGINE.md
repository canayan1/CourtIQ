# CourtIQ Mental Engine

## Purpose
The mental engine gives players practical routines and decision patterns for match focus, pressure, and consistency. It is not therapy; it is behavioral coaching for tennis performance.

## Core principles
- Tennis decisions are affected by routine, pressure, and momentum.
- Mental training should be concrete, repeatable, and connected to on-court behavior.
- Keep language performance-focused, not emotional.
- Build habits that make good decision-making automatic.

## Rituals for each pattern
- Pre-point routine: bounce twice, take a breath, set a target.
- Reset after an error: turn, reset grip, think about the next ball.
- One-point-at-a-time: verbalize "one point" before the serve.
- Momentum control: use the between-point walk to breathe and scan.
- Countdown breathing: inhale for 3, exhale for 3, then return to the ball.
- Positive self-talk: say "play the ball" instead of "don’t miss."
- Match plan recall: name one tactical priority before each game.
- Pressure acceptance: tell yourself "this is the point I wanted."
- Short-term memory reset: use the towel to create a clean break.
- Confidence through execution: focus on the process of the next shot.
- Patience under pressure: choose the safer option when the opponent is on the attack.
- Energy management: use easy hitting in practice to preserve focus for points.
- Visualizing the next point: picture the court target before the serve.
- Accepting conditions: note one condition fact and move on.
- Playing process over outcome: choose the option you would train.
- Tactical calmness: breathe out slowly on quiet moments.
- Composure after a bad call: reset with your routine, not with reaction.
- Maintaining aggression selectively: stay assertive only when the ball earns it.
- Reset on serve return games: use the first return to settle the body.
- Finishing strong: pick one clear target for the last game.

## Match modes
- Practice mode: focus on execution and routine, not score.
- Pressure mode: narrow the focus to one clear shot selection.
- Reset mode: use the between-point routine after every mistake.
- Momentum mode: recognize a swing and choose one safe option.

## Recommendation language
- Use action-oriented phrases: "Choose", "Set", "Reset", "Keep", "Focus".
- Avoid vague emotional terms: no "feeling better", no "confidence boost" without context.
- Keep advice tied to court behavior: "Use your first return to settle".
- Use confidence rules: stronger language for repeated correct choices, neutral language for marginal decisions.

## Practical use
- Mental guidance should show up as a short tip after the quiz result.
- Tips should be easy to follow on court the next time the player practices.
- Every mental tip should answer: "What can I do next time I am in that situation?"
