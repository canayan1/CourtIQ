## PART A — Content framework (the syllabus a category-leading tennis-IQ app should teach)

### Source caveat (bake into app tone)
Almost all rally-length and win-% data comes from **professional** match analysis. The _direction_ of the lessons (serve+1 matters, depth neutralizes, crosscourt is safer) holds at club level; the exact percentages do **not** transfer to a 3.0 player. Present numbers as "what the pros show us about _why_," never as club benchmarks. This is the #1 way tennis-IQ content goes wrong.

### A1. The spine: four court zones (governs all shot selection)
- **Defensive** (~6+ ft behind baseline, stretched): survive & reset — high, deep, crosscourt; recover.
- **Neutral** (0–2 ft behind baseline, balanced): build — crosscourt rally, depth, wait for the short ball/error.
- **Attacking** (inside baseline on a short ball): take time away — open court or approach.
- **Kill zone** (service line/net): finish — volley/overhead.
- **Master rule:** _match shot ambition to your zone. Most club errors are "offense from a defensive position."_

### A2. Tactical taxonomy by situation
- **Serving** — think **Serve+1** (the 3rd ball); divide each box into **Wide / Body / T**, always have a target; wide serve → open court ("big V" 2-1 pattern); **body serve jams** and is underused; first-serve **%** matters more than speed. ~70% of points end in ≤4 shots.
- **Returning** — two different shots: 1st serve = compact, deep, central (neutralize); 2nd serve = step in, drive/run-around. **Default target deep down the middle.** Goal = make the server hit a +1 from behind the baseline, not win outright.
- **Baseline rallying** — **crosscourt first** (most margin, lowest net, fastest recovery) until you earn a reason to change. **Change-of-direction rule (conditional!):** don't change direction on a good/deep/low ball — change on a short/high/central ball. Dial in **Height / Depth / Margin**. ~70/20/10 of points are 1–4 / 5–8 / 9+ shots. Patterns: **2-1** (deep→wide→open) and the **backhand cage**.
- **Defense / Neutral / Offense recognition** is the single biggest IQ jump for club players.
- **Approaching & net** — green lights: ball lands inside the service line **and** above net height, or opponent stretched wide. Approach deep (often DTL), split as they swing, cover the line you approached down.
- **Passing & lobbing** — primary pass = crosscourt roll (dips at the feet); low > hard; the **lob is a tactic, not panic** (esp. doubles); two-shot passing.

### A3. The 10 teachable IQ heuristics (the tag backbone for tips/quizzes/drills)
1. Play your zone (defense/neutral/offense). 2. Crosscourt until you get a ball to change DTL. 3. Recover to **bisect the angles** (not the center mark). 4. High-% under pressure, low-% when free. 5. Target the open court — or the body when there's none. 6. Attack the weak wing by **construction**, not repetition. 7. Net clearance is a strategy (aim a window above the net). 8. One purposeful shot beats two careless ones (errors lose more than winners win below 4.0). 9. The +1 mindset (every serve/return sets up the next ball). 10. When in doubt, deep & crosscourt.

### A4. DOUBLES strategy (priority — rich enough to be its own track)
- **Formations:** one-up-one-back (default, over-used); **both-up (the winning configuration — get both to net first)**; both-back (defensive vs big serve/lobs); **I-formation** (net partner centered & hidden, commits late; needs signals); **Australian** (server's partner same side, takes away crosscourt return; pairs with wide/lefty serve).
- **Serve + first volley:** serve to set up the poach/middle (body/T pull the return central); **first-volley target depends on height** — high → at the net player/open feet; **low/defensive → deep to the baseline returner or low middle, NOT reflexively at the net player.** Aim ~70% first serves in.
- **Middle/lobs/unit movement:** "down the middle solves the riddle" — pre-decide who takes the middle (forehand player); on a lob the **un-lobbed player calls "switch" and crosses**; move as a connected unit (rope between partners); net player's three jobs = poach the middle, protect the alley only on obvious wide balls, finish.
- **Poaching:** commit **just after the serve bounces, as the returner starts the forward swing**; poach off body/wide serves & soft returners; fake poaches keep them honest.
- **Signals & comms:** behind-the-back net-partner→server signals (open hand/finger = "I go", fist = "I stay") + serve placement; talk every point (switch/mine/yours/bounce-it). Communication + adaptability are non-negotiable.
- **Return (doubles):** crosscourt default; **keep it LOW at the volleyer's feet**; lob the aggressive net player; goal = get your team to the net.
- **Order/sides/lefty-righty:** each partner owns one side per set; steadier returner often on the **ad side** (more big points); **lefty plays the deuce side** so both forehands cover the middle; lead with the more reliable server.
- **Top club-doubles mistakes → fixes:** stuck one-up-one-back → move to both-up on a short ball; net player glued to the alley → start central, poach the middle; first volley auto-aimed at the net player → low volley deep/middle; floaty crosscourt returns → chip low/lob the poacher; no comms → signal every point; nobody covers middle → pre-assign the forehand player; both chase the lob → rotate as a unit; weak serve exposes partner → prioritize first-serve %.

### A5. Mental game / match IQ
- **Between-point routine — the 4 R's** (within ~25s): **Respond** (objective read, control body language) → **Relax** (physical reset: towel/strings/back fence/breathe) → **Re-focus/plan** (next-point pattern from opponent tendencies) → **Ready/ritual** (fixed pre-serve bounce/breath).
- **Pre-point routine:** identical ritual every serve/return; crowds out nerves.
- **Pressure:** routines are the antidote; slow down on big points; reduce to one clear high-% pattern; solutions-oriented self-talk.
- **Big points = high-% tennis** (break points, 30-30, deuce; 0-30 & 30-30 are hidden hinges). Serve your highest-confidence +1; return deep & in.
- **Scouting & adjusting:** find favorite patterns + disliked situations; note tendencies **ahead/behind/on big points**; exploit by construction; change a pattern that isn't working after a fair trial.

### A6. Level framework — teach the IQ appropriate to stroke reliability
| Level (NTRP / ~ITN) | Teach | Not yet |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner 2.0–2.5 / 10–9 | Keep ball in play; ready+split; get the serve in; recover to middle; "deep & crosscourt" as one idea; basic doubles positions + "mine/yours" | Serve+1, direction-change rules, poaching, percentages |
| Improver 3.0 / 8–7 | Crosscourt-first; 4 zones (defense vs offense); 1st/2nd-serve intent; deep neutral return; when (not how) to approach; basic reset routine; doubles: net on a short ball, don't drill the net player | Complex serve patterns, I/Australian, fine target zones |
| Intermediate 3.5 / 6 | Full zone shot-selection; change-direction rule + caveat; serve targets + serve+1; return targets; approach+volley as a unit; the 4-R routine; big-point = high-% ; doubles poaching/signals, both-up goal, low/lob returns | Heavy scouting systems, advanced I-formation gambits |
| Solid club 4.0 / 5 | Full pattern play (2-1, backhand cage); construct to a weakness; bisect-angle recovery; momentum/score management; scouting + in-match adjustment; full doubles toolkit | — (ceiling of core audience) |

**Pedagogy:** ITF Game Sense teaches tactics **game-first** — situation → choose → debrief the _why_. DropVolley's scenario-quiz format _is_ this model; lean into "situation → choose → why," each tied to a named heuristic.

### A7. Scenario-quiz design + example items
Each item = real situation + 3–4 plausible options (wrong ones = common club mistakes) + one best answer + the _why_ (framed around probability/court position, not absolutes) + tagged by situation & level. 12 vetted examples were produced (serve/return/rally/net/defense/recovery/doubles×4/mental×2) — see the research transcript; reproduce in the quiz schema during implementation.

### A8. Flags where coaching "rules" oversimplify (say so in copy)
1. "First to go DTL loses" is probabilistic — really "don't change direction on a _good_ ball." 2. Pro percentages teach _why_, not club benchmarks. 3. "Approach DTL always" — principle is "cover the line you approach down," crosscourt approach is right when they're wide. 4. One-up-one-back isn't "wrong" — it's the default + a valid defensive reset; the lesson is _transition to both-up_. 5. NTRP↔ITN↔UTR mapping is approximate — use tiers for content depth, not precise placement.

### Top gaps (vs a category-leading tennis-IQ app)
1. **Doubles strategy is almost absent** (the stated priority). Only the compatibility-analyzer prompt exists. Missing: doubles quiz scenarios, doubles drills, doubles tips (only ~3 of 50), doubles Pro Shot patterns (all 16 singles), and any doubles section in the AI manual.
2. **No tactical/technical training programs** — all 10 are gym/fitness. No on-court curricula (second-serve reliability, return, approach-and-volley, pattern rehearsal).
3. **Quiz depth/branching** — only 50 questions, all easy/medium, single-step (CLAUDE.md's "multi-step branching scenarios" don't exist). Practice caps at 8 Q/category (`Quiz.swift:251`).
4. **No beginner-tier (<2.5) scenario content** — AI manual targets 3.5–4.0; little for genuine beginners.
5. **Pro Shot + Court Tap carry almost no teaching copy** — coordinate data with one-liners; no "why/when/common mistake" depth.
