# Coach Review — deliverable template & worked example

*What the user buys, exactly what the coach delivers, and the quality bar.
Coach-facing (EN — coaches work in English first; Can is Founding Coach #1).*

## 1. What one review contains (the $14.99–19.99 promise)

| Piece | Spec | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice review** | 3–5 min audio, recorded over the video | The "real human" moment — warm, specific, references what they SEE |
| **Timestamped notes** | 5–8 notes pinned to video timestamps (`0:07 — racquet face open at contact`) | Skimmable proof of close watching |
| **Scorecard** | 5 checkpoints × 1–5 + one-line reason each | Comparable across reviews; feeds progress later |
| **Top-3 priorities** | Ranked; #1 = the single highest-leverage fix | Prevents feedback soup |
| **2 drills** | Named, court-or-wall doable, 10 min each, linked to priority #1/#2 | Actionable next session |
| **1 "keep doing"** | One genuine strength | Honesty includes positives; retention |

**SLA:** delivered within 72h. **Coach time target:** 15–20 min (AI case file
does the prep: impact count, per-stroke clips, draft observations).

## 2. Scorecard rubric (same 5 checkpoints as the measured swing pipeline)

1. **Preparation** — unit turn, early racquet prep, spacing to ball
2. **Contact zone** — contact point vs body, racquet face, extension
3. **Kinetic chain** — legs→hips→torso→arm sequencing, energy transfer
4. **Finish & recovery** — follow-through shape, balance at end, reset step
5. **Footwork & balance** — split step, adjustment steps, base width

Score anchors: 1 = fundamental rebuild · 3 = club-solid, inconsistent ·
5 = strength to build tactics around. Never inflate; a 3 with a clear reason
is worth more than a kind 4.

## 3. Tone & boundary rules

- Specific over general: cite the timestamp, describe what's visible.
- One idea per note. No jargon without a 5-word translation.
- **Never**: injury diagnosis, medical advice, equipment upselling,
  guarantees ("this will add 10 mph").
- If video quality/angle blocks judgment on a checkpoint: SAY SO, score it
  `–`, and include the filming tip for next time (honesty > completeness).

## 4. Worked example (forehand, club player, 34s clip, 6 strokes)

**Voice review (outline the coach speaks, ~4 min):**
"Hi! Watched your six forehands a few times — here's what stands out. Big
positive first: your contact height management is genuinely good, you take the
ball comfortably between waist and chest on 5 of 6… The one thing I'd change
first is your unit turn timing: at 0:07 and again at 0:19 the racquet starts
back only as the ball crosses the net — that's late, and it's why the two
shots at 0:19 and 0:27 were rushed and landed short… Second priority is the
recovery step: after contact you admire the shot for a beat (0:12) instead of
split-stepping back… For this week: shadow-swing drill with an early-turn cue,
and the wall two-touch rhythm drill — details in the notes. Overall this is a
solid club forehand with one timing fix away from being a weapon."

**Timestamped notes:**
- `0:04` Nice wide base at the split — keep this.
- `0:07` Racquet prep starts as ball crosses the net → late. Cue: "turn as the
  opponent hits".
- `0:12` Finish is complete, but you hold the pose ~1s — recovery step missing.
- `0:19` Late prep again → contact behind ideal point, ball lands short.
- `0:23` Best forehand of the set: early turn, clean chain — THIS is the model.
- `0:27` Rushed swing; compare with 0:23 side by side.

**Scorecard:** Preparation **2** (late trigger) · Contact **4** (good height,
occasionally behind) · Kinetic chain **3** (clean when on time) · Finish &
recovery **3** (finish good, recovery absent) · Footwork **3** (good base,
few adjustment steps).

**Top-3:** 1) Early unit turn (trigger cue) · 2) Recovery split after finish ·
3) Adjustment steps on short balls.

**Drills:** 1) *Shadow early-turn* — 3×10 shadow swings, saying "turn" the
moment the (imaginary) opponent hits; then 20 wall balls same cue.
2) *Wall two-touch rhythm* — wall rally, deliberate bounce-hit rhythm call,
20 in a row without rushing.

**Keep doing:** contact-height discipline — genuinely above club average.

## 5. Delivery workflow (P0 concierge)

1. Order arrives (push + dashboard row) with AI case file attached.
2. Coach watches full clip once, then per-stroke clips.
3. Fills scorecard + notes in dashboard → records voice memo last (notes make
   the voice tighter).
4. Hits Deliver → user push: "Your coach review is ready 🎾".
5. Auto-rating prompt to user after viewing.

## 6. P0 CHOSEN FORMAT — the $20 "One Thing" review (low text, high signal)

*Supersedes §1 quantities for launch. Owner is Founding Coach #1 (anonymous
in-product: "a real tennis coach" — no name, no identity). Design goal:
skimmable in 30 seconds, accurate, produced in 12–15 min.*

**The deliverable, in reading order:**

1. **Scorecard** — 5 bars (Preparation · Contact · Chain · Finish · Footwork),
   1–5, no prose. Un-judgeable from this angle → `–` (honesty beats coverage).
2. **THE ONE THING** — single highest-leverage fix. Exactly:
   1 sentence + 1 timestamp + 1 cue word.
   > "Your racquet starts back too late — watch 0:07. Cue: 'turn as they hit'."
3. **3 micro-notes** — ≤10 words each, every one timestamped:
   1 strength ✅ + 2 fault moments ⚠️. Proof of close watching, zero fluff.
4. **1 drill** — trains THE ONE THING. Name + 2 lines + rep count.
5. **Voice (2–3 min)** — speaks ONLY the four items above, warm and personal
   (their name, their clip moments). Voice = warmth; text = signal.

**Word budget: ≤100 written words total.** If it doesn't fit, the review
isn't finished being prioritized.

**Why one fix, not three:** a $20 rec-player review earns repeat purchase
when the player can DO something tomorrow and see the difference. Three
priorities compete; one priority converts. (Next review naturally continues:
"last time we fixed X — now Y.")

**Accuracy rules (unchanged):** claim only what's visible; timestamp every
claim; say "can't judge from this angle" openly; never medical.
