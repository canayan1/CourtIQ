Scene 1 (0.0–1.0s): cream ground; a thin sand vertical rail draws downward

Scene 2 (1.0–3.0s): stop one lands on the rail: "2–4 HOURS BEFORE" in clay
small caps, and beneath it "A meal built on carbohydrate." Long-tail settle,
no bounce.
Scene 3 (3.0–4.6s): stop two: "1 HOUR OUT" / "A banana, toast or oatmeal."

the most room: "EVERY CHANGEOVER" / "Four to twelve swallows — to a plan,

clay underline draws beneath the word CHANGEOVER as it settles.
Scene 5 (7.4–10.0s): stop four: "PAST THE FIRST HOUR" / "30 to 60 g of
carbohydrate an hour." Then the whole rail holds still and reads. No camera
move anywhere in this frame — the cascade is the motion.

## Video direction

The reel teaches before it sells. Two thirds of its running time is the
clock itself, and a viewer who never installs still leaves knowing when to
eat and what to do at a changeover — that is the point, and it is also why
the piece can be calm.

The changeover stop is the centre of gravity. It is the only moment in the
list that belongs to tennis and to nothing else, it is the one club players
ignore, and it gets more time and a step more size than its neighbours.

Rhythm: ink, then cream, then cream. One ground change, at the moment the
ad stops talking about the player's problem and starts answering it.

No camera moves anywhere in this piece. Every reveal is a settle on a
long-tail curve; nothing bounces, nothing glows, no stock footage. The BGM
is a quiet bed with no build and no drop.

Every number on screen is in the shipped guide with a source behind it. If
a number cannot be traced there, it does not go on a frame.
