Adapt: keep the held-device-as-hero signature and the continuous camera
push, but the screen does not cycle — it is one screen, and the move is a
single slow push onto one card inside it.

Scene 1 (0.0–1.4s): cream ground; the phone rises from below into the lower
two-thirds, settling on a long-tail curve, thin ink hairline frame, no gloss
— Centered, the device at ~70% of frame height, 3 depth layers (ground ·
soft contact shadow · device).
Scene 2 (1.4–4.2s): one continuous slow push-in toward the "WHAT WORKS FOR
YOU" card near the bottom of the screen. The push never stops or restarts;
by the end the card fills most of the width and the four averages are
legible.
Scene 3 (4.2–6.4s): the push settles. A clay highlight wipes left to right
beneath the refusal sentence — "…at least 5 sessions on each side to mean
anything." — at reading speed, not faster.
Scene 4 (6.4–8.0s): "It counts before it talks." fades up in clay across the
lower third, over a dimmed base. Everything else holds dead still.

The payoff, and the only frame with a camera move. The real capture slides
up inside a thin phone frame, then the camera pushes slowly in on the
"WHAT WORKS FOR YOU" card until the line fills the width:

"About 7 more rated sessions until your first comparison. Each one needs at
least 5 sessions on each side to mean anything."

A clay highlight wipes under that sentence as it settles. Overlay, clay,
bottom third: "It counts before it talks."

Eight seconds is deliberate. The sentence has to be readable, and a viewer
who reads it has already been convinced — the screen does the arguing.
