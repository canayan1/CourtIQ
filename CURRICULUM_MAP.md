# CourtIQ Curriculum Map

## Purpose
This curriculum map connects quiz mistakes to practical performance and mental guidance without overclaiming. It supports coaching recommendations that are grounded in tennis practice.

- forcing under pressure

- being too passive
- forcing the net

- staying on the same path

## Mistake → performance mapping
- `forcing the second serve` → practice second-serve margin and recovery drills.
- `being predictable` → add variety to serve placement and court movement.
- `serving into strength` → focus on serve target selection and opponent read.
- `overaggressive serving` → choose higher-percentage serve options when the score is tight.
- `ignoring returner positioning` → train court awareness and opponent reaction before serving.
- `forcing under pressure` → build composure and safe decision-making in pressured serves.
- `serving too central` → practice wide and body serve targets.
- `losing serve rhythm` → use consistent pre-serve routine and simplified motion.
- `attacking the wrong wing` → practice opponent scouting and targeting weak shots.
- `overplaying with a lead` → emphasize stability and shot selection when ahead.
- `underestimating body serves` → use serve-to-body drills and shorter follow-up patterns.
- `waiting too long` → train on short ball attack and early decision-making.
- `overextending` → focus on recovery and safe return placement.
- `following the same path` → practice direction changes and alternative patterns.
- `overreaching` → practice safer options in tense games.
- `staying on one pattern` → develop pattern variation through repeated drills.
- `missing the weak reply` → train anticipation and immediate attack on short balls.
- `playing too defensively` → balance defense with controlled aggression.
- `being too passive` → practice finishing options at the net.
- `forcing the net` → reserve net approaches for strong opportunities.
- `reacting emotionally` → build a reset routine between points.
- `staying on the same path` → break patterns with alternate shot selection.

## Mistake → mental mapping
- `forcing the second serve` → use calm pre-point breathing and trust a safer serve.
- `being predictable` → remind yourself to vary your next choice.
- `serving into strength` → note opponent positioning before you serve.
- `overaggressive serving` → slow down and choose the safer ball.
- `ignoring returner positioning` → scan the returner’s stance before the toss.
- `forcing under pressure` → focus on the process over the point outcome.
- `serving too central` → use a target rather than a feeling.
- `losing serve rhythm` → return to your routine and simplify your motion.
- `attacking the wrong wing` → choose the option you trained in practice.
- `overplaying with a lead` → think "keep the lead" not "try to finish it."
- `underestimating body serves` → prepare for a short or angled reply.
- `waiting too long` → remind yourself when an opening appears.
- `overextending` → stay with a recovery shot before attacking.
- `following the same path` → mentally commit to a new direction.
- `overreaching` → choose the safer option when the score is tight.
- `staying on one pattern` → say "change it" before your serve or return.
- `missing the weak reply` → expect the short ball and attack it.
- `playing too defensively` → choose a controlled aggression shot.
- `being too passive` → move forward only when you have a clear target.
- `forcing the net` → remind yourself to approach only when the ball earns it.
- `reacting emotionally` → use a reset routine after mistakes.
- `staying on the same path` → choose a second pattern before the point begins.

## Recommendation logic
- If a quiz item is wrong, recommend a drill that addresses the mistake’s physical or tactical cause.
- If the quiz item is right, reinforce the safer option and suggest a small follow-up habit.
- Use confidence rules:
  - correct answers on repeated patterns → "Your decision is aligning with good tennis sense."
  - incorrect answers on high-risk choices → "This is a place to simplify and protect the point."
  - mixed results in the same category → "Build consistency by repeating the same category practice."

## Confidence rules
- 0–2 correct: emphasize basic stability and routine.
- 3–4 correct: emphasize pattern awareness and clean execution.
- 5 correct: reinforce decision-making and consistency.

## Notes
- Keep the curriculum mapping conservative: avoid saying "this will make you win".
- Focus on what the player can train next, not on perfect outcomes.
